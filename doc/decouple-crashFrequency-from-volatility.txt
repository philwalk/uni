Decouple crash frequency from volatility. It's one mechanism, it's measurable, and it's the specific thing that made this sweep level-blind.

  Model elasticity of crash count to volatility: ln(43.63/19.95) / ln(25.05/15.76) = 1.69. Real, from QQQ against the S&P (26.9%/25.6 vs 16.0%/20.7): 0.41, consistent with the ~0.50 cross-section the uni docs cite. A 4× gap.

  Read the consequence straight off the table: QQQ's crash rate wants depth ≈ 16, which gives a 17.2% market — an S&P, not a Nasdaq. QQQ's volatility wants depth ≈ 8–9, which gives 50+ crashes per century, double the real rate. The pair (26.9% vol, 25.6 crashes/century) is unreachable at any depth. That's why
  the recipe ships with crashes/century 1.69× disclosed as a miss rather than tuned away — it's structural, not a parameter someone left wrong.

  Why it's the binding one for this sweep

  The jump model's bear state is a cluster in (downside deviation, fast Sortino, slow Sortino) space, so bear share is essentially crash frequency × crash duration. The 1.7× crash-rate miss maps almost one-for-one onto the 2–4× bear-share inflation, which is exactly what makes λ's level untransferable. None of
  the other recorded gaps touch it — the model is single-asset and close-only, so the missing cross-section and the missing intraday bars are irrelevant here.

  It's also not one row. The same column drags kurtosis (1.62× at depth 10 → 1.33× at depth 16) and clustering (0.36 → 0.32, against a 0.40 realism ceiling). One mechanism change closes three of the four disclosed misses at Nasdaq volatility.

  What the change would be

  Volatility in this model is bought almost entirely through depth — depth sets price impact, impact drives the liquidity-withdrawal cascade, and the cascade is the crash. The Nasdaq recipe makes this vivid: it reaches 25% volatility by cutting depth 17.4 → 10 while lowering jumpvar 0.17 → 0.02 and fundvol
  0.07 → 0.06. Every point of extra volatility comes from more spirals.

  The missing piece is a volatility channel that adds no cascades. The natural candidate is the frozen equity noise constant sigmaN (0.007, ~11% annualised on its own), promoted from a frozen constant to a World field — a purely diffusive dial, so a high-volatility world can be built from noise instead of from
  more frequent spirals. Promoting a frozen constant is a move the upgrade plan already treats as legitimate (W7 does exactly that), and it defaults to bit-identical behaviour at today's value.

  The falsifiable test: at depth ≈ 16 (crash rate matched) with sigmaN raised to reach 26.9%, does the world pass the gate with crashes ≈ 25.6 and kurtosis nearer 9.55? If volatility from noise doesn't inflate the crash count, the elasticity is fixed and λ becomes readable in levels.

  Meanwhile, a workaround you can use today

  Score on a crash-rate-matched world instead of a volatility-matched one — depth 16, accepting 17.2% volatility. For a detector that clusters on downside deviation, crash frequency is what sets bear share, so matching crashes and missing volatility is likely the better of the two available misspecifications.
