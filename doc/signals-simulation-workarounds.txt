### Key Technical Concerns & Workarounds

#### 1. §2b Block Bootstrap: Seam Jumps and Destroyed Lead-Lag Horizon

* **The Problem:**
* *Discontinuities:* Resampling non-stationary levels (like credit spreads) creates massive step-function jumps at block boundaries (e.g., $5.5\% \rightarrow 1.8\%$).
* *Block Length:* A 20–60 day block destroys macro leads (like yield curve inversions that lead recessions by 6–18 months). Resampling at 18-month block lengths (~378 days) leaves only ~23 independent historical blocks, turning the test into a variance-reduction tool rather than a true path generator.


* **Workarounds:**
* **Stationary Bootstrap on Differenced Series:** Resample 1-day differences ($\Delta x_t$) using a **Stationary Bootstrap** (Politis & Romano) with exponentially distributed block lengths. Re-integrate levels using a **Vector Error Correction / Mean-Reverting process** (like an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck drift back toward historical medians) to prevent random walks into negative spreads or impossible rates.
* **Cross-Sectional Smooth Anchoring at Seams:** Apply a tapering window (e.g., 5-day linear blend) on the boundary differences to eliminate abrupt jump artifacts.
* **Acknowledge Sample Limitations:** Accept that resampling large macro cycles from 35 years of data yields low independent sample power (~20–25 macro cycles), and frame the test strictly as a *path-order variance test* rather than an out-of-sample generator.



#### 2. §2a MS-VAR & §4 GARCH/Copula: Contemporaneous vs. Lead-Lag Alignment

* **The Problem:**
* A standard t-copula couples *contemporaneous* residuals, meaning macro stress and market crashes arrive on the exact same day. Because the strategy needs prior warning to exit, a purely contemporaneous model forces the timing strategy to fail by design.
* Mixed data frequencies (daily rates vs. weekly credit vs. quarterly GDP) cause severe missing-data issues in standard VARs.


* **Workarounds:**
* **Cross-Correlation Lagged Copula / VAR-GARCH:** If using Copulas, embed them in a **VAR(p)-GARCH** framework where macro features at time $t - k$ explicitly drive conditional return/volatility distributions at time $t$.
* **State-Space / MIDAS Modeling:** Address mixed frequencies using a **State-Space Kalman Filter** or **MIDAS (Mixed Data Sampling)** regression to treat weekly/quarterly observations as latent daily processes observed with lag and noise.
* **Asymmetric Vine Copulas:** Replace the symmetric t-copula with an **Asymmetric C-Vine or D-Vine Copula** using Clayton/Gumbel pair-copulas to explicitly model the asymmetric tail dependence (joint market/credit crashes without joint rallies).



#### 3. §3 HMM: Conditional Independence and Signal Distortion

* **The Problem:** Standard HMMs assume feature emissions are conditionally independent given the state. In reality, credit spreads, yield curves, and volatility co-move tightly during stress. Emitting them independently produces near-binomial vote distributions that ruin the smoothed stress count's persistence.
* **Workarounds:**
* **Multivariate Normal / Copula Emissions:** Do not emit individual votes independently. Instead, parameterize each HMM state with a full **multivariate joint emission distribution** (covariances included) across all features.
* **Use Exclusively as a Null Generator:** Treat the HMM as a benchmark generator for the **No-Edge Null Distribution** (destroying the price-macro link intentionally to test if the gate picks up false-positive signals from noise).



#### 4. Revision & Cadence Layers (Applies to All Methods)

* **The Problem:** Models fit to final revised data give the strategy unfair future information (e.g., revised leverage metrics or un-lagged quarterly reports).
* **Workarounds:**
* **ALFRED Revision-Noise Injector:** Estimate the empirical distribution of vintage revision errors from ALFRED ($\text{Revision}_t = \text{Final}_t - \text{Published}_t$). Superimpose this noise onto synthetic series and enforce explicit publication lag buffers (e.g., 1-day lag for rates, 7-day lag for weekly series, 45-day lag for macro indices) before feeding inputs to the model.
